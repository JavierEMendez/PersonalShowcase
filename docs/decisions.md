# Decisions

Dated entries. One paragraph each: what, why, what was rejected. Newest at the bottom.

## 2026-09-14 · Two products, one site
Showcase two things under one domain: the existing MPC Underwriting tool (ported from an internal tool) and a new Deal-to-Portfolio Copilot. One product alone covered too few of the target roles; the pair covers credit, M&A operations, business modeling, and CRE strategy. Rejected: a narrower Houston MPC land market model, and a portfolio monitoring tool on its own.

## 2026-09-14 · One Railway app, one domain
Landing page and both tools live in a single FastAPI app with routes /underwriting and /copilot. One link to send, one codebase to maintain. Rejected: a static landing page linking to separately hosted apps.

## 2026-09-14 · Synthetic deals with public market data
Demos run on invented deals (Cypress Ridge, Harbor Point Industrial) with realistic Houston-area figures and public data sources. Nothing from the internal book, no employer branding or template files. Rejected: anonymized internal deals (needs sign-off) and SEC-filings-only deals (weak tie to MPC land).

## 2026-09-14 · Describe the MPC tool as an unlevered model
The internal tool's documentation claims debt terms and equity multiple; its engine has neither. The showcase describes the tool accurately: unlevered XIRR, gross and net margin, per-acre and per-lot metrics. Adding a financing layer to the MPC tool is deferred.

## 2026-09-14 · Design standard locked from mockups
Executive, bank-style standard: warm near-white ground, charcoal ink, navy accent, Source Serif 4 and IBM Plex Sans, tabular numerals, 2px radii, no shadows or gradients, eight-figure summary strip on every tool screen. Rejected: reusing the employer theme (employer branding on a personal showcase) and Inter (overused).

## 2026-09-14 · Wordmark
"Javier Mendez Valdez" with a small headshot at the top left of every page, confirmed by Javi. Superseded the working wordmark "Mendez Valdez".

## 2026-09-14 · Harbor Point priced as a bid below ask
The Copilot sample deal recommends a $38.0M bid against a $41.6M ask, because at the ask the levered IRR is 4.9% against a 12% threshold. Shows the tool producing a pricing decision rather than confirming the seller's number. Rejected: a deal that simply clears the hurdle at the ask.

## 2026-09-14 · Fixed 1440px page width
Pages render at the 1440px design width on every device (viewport meta set to 1440, page container 1440px centered) instead of a responsive layout. The reader is a partner on a laptop; on a phone the page scales down and reads like the PNG render rather than reflowing dense tables into a single column. Rejected: a responsive grid, which would need a second layout for every table and panel.

## 2026-09-14 · Contact email from the environment
The About section shows an email row only when the CONTACT_EMAIL environment variable is set on Railway. Keeps a personal address out of the public repository and lets it change without a commit. Rejected: hardcoding the address in the template.

## 2026-09-14 · Docker build on Railway
Railway builds from the repository Dockerfile (python:3.12-slim, pinned requirements.txt) with a /health check, rather than Nixpacks autodetection. The build is reproducible and the same image runs locally. Rejected: Nixpacks, which picks the Python version and start command implicitly.

## 2026-09-14 · Engine ported and reconciled; Cypress Ridge figures now come from the port
`core/underwriting/` is a module-by-module port of the internal land model, with typed pydantic inputs and the calculation order preserved. On the Cypress Ridge inputs it reproduces the internal engine to the dollar on every summary line, every monthly cash flow row, and the XIRR to ten decimals, for all three scenarios. The reference outputs are frozen in `tests/fixtures/cypress_ridge.json`. Inputs the deals document left open were set as model defaults or realistic Houston figures; section development cost was calibrated to $1,020 per front foot ($580 water, sewer and drainage, $440 paving) so the deal lands near the intended return. The port's headline figures replace the hand-built mockup figures: unlevered IRR 17.4% (mockup 18.4%), total revenue $379.1M (412.6), gross costs $291.7M (318.4), gross margin $87.4M or 23.1% of revenue (94.2M, 22.8%), net margin $72.5M (71.5M), 2,380 lots (2,412), 101 months (134), peak cash need $87.1M in month 37 (92.2M, month 34), breakeven in year 6 (year 8). Faster pace runs 19.5% and Lower lot price 11.9%; the sensitivity grid spans 10.1% to 25.9%. The largest line differences: MUD and WCID proceeds $112.3M against 46.1M in the mockup, because the 12% and 4.2% debt ratios apply to about $800M of assessed value; lot sales $235.6M against 318.9M on 2,380 lots at $1,800 per front foot; collector roads $8.8M against 22.6M on 18.6 acres of right of way. Rejected: forcing inputs to hit the mockup numbers. `docs/synthetic-deals.md` is updated to the fixture in build step 3.

## 2026-09-14 · Two source-model behaviours kept as is
Two behaviours of the internal engine were ported unchanged so the numbers reconcile, and are flagged here rather than fixed. First, the last home-sale month that closes the marketing, insurance and bookkeeping windows is taken from the last lot size processed, rather than the latest across all sizes; on Cypress Ridge every size sells out in the same month so nothing changes. Second, empty collector road rows keep their default start months and a six-month build, so an otherwise empty tract still reports a 101-month project and Cypress Ridge runs to month 101 rather than 98. Rejected: changing either in the port, which would break the reference reconciliation before the web layer exists. Both are candidates for a later, separately reconciled fix.

## 2026-09-14 · Copilot refocused on multifamily; Harbor Point Industrial replaced by Sawyer Bend Apartments
The second product is now the Multifamily Copilot. Its model follows the structure of a private multifamily buyer's model (inventoried in the gitignored `private/multifamily-model-inventory.md`): rent roll by floor plan to market rent with loss-to-lease burn-off, a unit renovation program with premiums, a per-unit operating budget with real estate taxes reassessed at purchase, an agency-style loan sized on the lesser of LTV, DSCR and debt yield with an interest-only period, exit on forward NOI, and an LP/GP waterfall with a preferred return and tiered promote. The synthetic sample deal is Sawyer Bend Apartments, a 288-unit garden community built in 2016 in Northwest Houston, replacing Harbor Point Industrial; its hand-built figures are in `docs/synthetic-deals.md` and, as with Cypress Ridge, the model's outputs replace them in build step 4. Landing navigation reads Land Underwriting and Multifamily Copilot. The copilot mockup in `design/` keeps its panel layout as the reference; its industrial content is superseded. Rejected: keeping a generic income-property copilot, which matched no single hiring conversation as well as multifamily does.

## 2026-09-14 · Site mark and wordmark
JM monogram in Source Serif 4 Medium, page ground on a navy square, with the wordmark "Javier Mendez" so initials and name agree. Two initials read at 16 px and give the browser tab a solid block where the default globe was. The lockup replaces the headshot and full-name wordmark used earlier the same day; the photo stays in the repository for a later About-section use. Rejected: MV (initials should be the person's), a single J, a circular seal (ring disappears at 16 px), and a sans version.

## 2026-09-14 · Land Underwriting screen: scenarios live in a browser session, rather than a database
Edits on the Tract, Costs, Revenue and Lookups tabs recalculate the active scenario and are held in a server-side session keyed by a cookie, expiring after a day. Nothing is written to Postgres in this release: the public demo needs no login and no saving, and a redeploy returning every visitor to the seed deal is the right behaviour for a showcase. Scenarios can be added, renamed, removed, and compared side by side; Main is the seed and the only one that cannot be removed. Rejected: persisting anonymous sessions to the database (adds a table, migrations and cleanup jobs for no reader-visible benefit).

## 2026-09-14 · Excel export: schedule as values, arithmetic as live formulas
The export is built from scratch with openpyxl and ships four sheets: Inputs, Summary, Pro forma (monthly), Sensitivity. The monthly line items are engine values; every total, net, cumulative, margin, ratio and the XIRR on the Summary sheet is a live formula over them, so a reviewer can audit the arithmetic and trace each headline to the months behind it. The landing page copy was tightened to say so. Rejected: rebuilding the full land model as spreadsheet formulas (thousands of cells to maintain in parallel with the Python engine) and shipping the internal workbook template (not ours to publish).

## 2026-09-14 · Sensitivity grid recalculates on the server
The grid reruns the engine for every cell (3 ms per run, at most 49 cells) when the axes change, through one HTMX request. Six drivers are offered: lot price per front foot, pace, contingency, home price, yield, and development cost per lot; metric is unlevered IRR or gross margin. Cell shading ranks values across the visible grid. Rejected: precomputing grids or shading on fixed thresholds.

## 2026-09-14 · Cypress Ridge documentation now matches the fixture
`docs/synthetic-deals.md` carries the engine's figures for Cypress Ridge (acreage, summary, financial summary, yearly cash flow, sensitivity), generated from `tests/fixtures/cypress_ridge.json` and the seed. The hand-built mockup figures are gone from the docs; the mockup PNGs in `design/` still show them and remain the layout reference. The landing page reads its sample figures from the engine at startup, so the two cannot drift.

## 2026-09-14 · Multifamily Copilot direction: a working intake, with the buyer's-model port as the calculator
The Copilot is built to be used, not only shown. Its backbone is the multifamily engine ported in build step 4 from the structure in `private/multifamily-model-inventory.md`. The Screen step becomes an intake: the user uploads a broker offering memorandum and, when available, a rent roll and a trailing twelve-month operating statement; extraction returns every model input it can find with a source citation and a confidence grade; the tool then lists what the documents did not settle and asks for it, one question per missing or low-confidence input (renovation scope and premium, tax reassessment basis, insurance quote, loan terms, exit cap, hold period), with the extracted figure and its source shown next to each question so the analyst can accept or override. Answers flow into the typed inputs and the Underwrite step runs from there. The demo ships with synthetic documents for Sawyer Bend so the intake can be exercised without an API key; the live extraction path needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. Phasing: build step 4 engine and tests; step 5 screen from the seed; step 6 uploads, extraction, evals and the question loop; step 7 memo. Rejected: a free-form chat over the documents (no auditable link from a figure to its source) and extraction without the question loop (an OM never carries the buyer's assumptions, so the model would silently fill them).

## 2026-09-14 · Multifamily engine built; Sawyer Bend figures now come from the engine
`core/copilot/` is a monthly engine reported annually, following the private inventory: rent roll by floor plan to market with loss to lease burning off over twelve months, a renovation program with pace, downtime and premiums, revenue deductions, per-unit expenses stepped yearly with taxes reassessed at the price, management fee on EGI, reserves, a loan sized on the lesser of LTV, DSCR and debt yield with interest only then amortization, sources and uses with capital funded at close, exit on the twelve forward months of NOI, monthly XIRR, and a three-tier LP/GP waterfall on monthly accrual balances. The engine's figures replace the hand-built annual figures: levered IRR 14.8% (was 13.5%), unlevered 10.2%, equity multiple 1.89× (1.81×), LP IRR 13.0% (12.4%), going-in cap 5.74% (5.81%), year 1 NOI $2.64M, exit value $62.8M (61.3M), levered IRR at the ask 7.9% (6.8%). The lift comes from monthly compounding of market rents and an exit on forward NOI, which the annual scratch model approximated. Inputs did not change. The reference outputs are frozen in `tests/fixtures/sawyer_bend.json`. Rejected: tuning inputs to reproduce the scratch figures.

## 2026-09-14 · Copilot underwrite screen built from the engine
`/copilot` renders every panel from `core/copilot` at request time: the eight-cell strip, NOI by year, rent roll and renovation program, extracted assumptions (seeded in `data/sawyer_bend.json` until build step 6 makes them live), sources and uses with loan terms and the three sizing constraints, the single-variable stress table, the IC memo, the waterfall, the Monitor panel and a case comparison. Case pills switch Base, Downside and Lender through a query parameter; each case exports its own workbook. The IC memo is drafted from model figures only, with a fixed 12% levered-IRR threshold deciding bid or pass, so the Downside case reads as a pass; build step 7 replaces the sentence templates with the language model without changing where the figures come from. The Monitor panel shows a synthetic second quarter after closing (DSCR and debt yield slightly above underwriting, renovations three units behind plan) so the covenant-test layout can be judged before real actuals exist. Rejected: a per-browser session store for Copilot cases, since the screen has no editable inputs until the intake arrives.

## 2026-09-14 · Editable numbers are text inputs with thousands separators
Input fields render as text with `inputmode="decimal"` and show separators above 999 (50,000 rather than 50000.0); the form parser already strips commas, dollar signs and percent signs on the way in. Browser number inputs cannot display separators, and the loss of spin buttons is acceptable on a model form. Percent fields still show percentages and store fractions.

## 2026-09-14 · Layout: two-column grids nest, twelve-column grids do not
Panels placed side by side inside a column use `.grid-2`; the earlier nested `.grid-12` collapsed its children to one column each because only some span classes existed. Wide input tables sit in a horizontal scroll container so a panel never pushes past its column. The About section stays at the bottom of the landing page and `/about` carries the full bio with the headshot.

## 2026-09-14 · Screen intake: structured files parsed, the OM read, every figure cited and checked
The Screen step takes three uploads (offering memorandum as PDF; rent roll and T-12 as xlsx or csv), reads them in memory with size and type limits, and returns a catalogue of fifteen figures with document, page or rows, a verbatim quote and a confidence grade. The rent roll and T-12 are parsed deterministically because their figures are sums and averages over rows, which a language model should not be asked to do. The OM is prose and tables; it is read by the Claude API through a typed tool call when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set and by a rule reader (regular expressions over the page text) otherwise, so the demo runs without a key. Every OM extraction is verified: the quote must appear on the cited page and must contain the figure, or the extraction is downgraded to Low and marked unverified, whichever reader produced it. Confidence grades follow one rule: facts about the property are High, broker estimates, surveys, comparables and seller claims are Medium, seller-side figures that will not survive the sale (taxes on the old assessment) are Low. The synthetic package in `data/sawyer_bend/` is generated from the seed inputs so the eval in `evals/screen/` can score the reader; the rule reader scores fifteen of fifteen by construction, and the number that matters is the model reader on unfamiliar OMs, which needs real packages. Rejected: sending the rent roll rows to the model (slow, costly and wrong in aggregate), and extraction without verification (a confident wrong citation is worse than a blank).

## 2026-09-14 · Question loop: ask what the documents cannot settle, default to the best figure available
Nineteen questions cover the inputs an OM never carries (the bid, hold period, renovation scope and cost, rent growth, vacancy, reserves and all seven loan terms) plus any catalogue figure that came back below High (the renovation premium, the exit cap, the seller's insurance and controllable expenses, the tax reassessment basis). Each question is prefilled with the extracted figure when there is one and the current model input otherwise, and shows the source and the model input side by side so accepting a seller figure is a visible choice. Answers and document facts (floor plans, occupancy, year built, asking price, tax rate, other income, expense lines) land on the sample deal's base inputs and open as a Screened case beside Base, Downside and Lender, per browser session, with its own export. Controllable expenses are asked as one per-unit figure that scales the seven extracted lines proportionally. Rejected: a free-form chat over the documents, and hiding questions whose default equals the model input (the point is to show what was assumed).

## 2026-09-14 · IC memo: the writer places holders, the code places figures
`core/copilot/memo.py` builds a facts table from the engine output (about thirty formatted figures keyed by name, such as `{levered_irr}` = 14.8%) and asks the writer for prose that uses those names in braces wherever a number belongs. The writer is a sentence template by default and the Claude API when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is set. A draft is rejected when it contains a digit, an unknown placeholder, a banned phrase, a question mark, fewer than three or more than six body sentences, fewer than three or more than five items in "what the model cannot tell you", or a recommendation that disagrees with the threshold test (bid when the levered IRR clears 12%, pass when it does not). A rejected model draft falls back to the template and the memo says so. A second pass on the finished text confirms that every number in it is a facts value. This makes the requirement that every figure come from the model output dict a property of the construction, rather than a check on the language model's arithmetic. The eval in `evals/memo/` runs the three seeded cases through the same checks; a fallback counts as a failure for the model writer. Rejected: letting the model write figures and verifying them afterwards (a verified wrong figure is still a wrong figure a reader saw), and free-form prose without a schema (the tool call gives typed sections).

## 2026-09-14 · Redirection: the Copilot's calculator will be a faithful port of the buyer's model
Direction from the owner: the multifamily and build-to-rent tool should work like the MPC tool, as a Python version of the internal Excel buyer's model (`private/multifamily-model-inventory.md` describes its 33 sheets), with recommendations and the memo built on top; the intake takes an OM, a rent roll and an optional T-12 and fills the underwriting as far as the documents allow before the analyst answers the rest. The engine in `core/copilot/` built in step 4 followed the workbook's structure from the inventory; it is not a cell-for-cell port and does not reproduce the workbook's figures. It is kept until the port lands and is then replaced. Plan: step 8 ports the workbook sheet by sheet into typed inputs and modules with the same calculation order (rent roll and floor plans, unit type summary, rent analysis, turnover and rollover, interior renovation, physical upgrades, capital improvement summary, operations and monthly cash flows, RET and payroll and contract services and marketing, acquisition loan, refinance loan, closing costs, reserves, property returns, waterfall, summary), reconciled to the workbook's own values on the internal deal through a private script that is never committed, with zero mismatches as the bar, exactly as `calc.py` was reconciled for the MPC tool. Step 9 re-points Screen, Underwrite and the memo at the ported inputs and outputs, with input tabs that mirror the workbook's sheets the way the MPC tool's tabs do. The workbook itself, its deal figures and its defined names never enter the repo; the seeded deal stays Sawyer Bend, synthetic. Rejected: keeping the structural engine as the calculator (it would never match the model the analysts trust) and porting only the sheets the screen uses (partial ports drift).

## 2026-09-14 · Port declined; original plan resumed
The redirection above was reconsidered the same day and withdrawn. A cell-level port of a 33-sheet workbook is eight to twelve sessions of formula reading whose result, fidelity to a model the audience will never open, is invisible on a public site and adds nothing at work, where the workbook stays the tool. The Copilot keeps the engine built in step 4, which follows the workbook's structure on synthetic data, and the remaining plan is the original one: README and case study. Cheaper follow-ups that keep most of the benefit are logged under Deferred: a private calibration of the engine against the workbook by entering the Sawyer Bend inputs into it and comparing outputs; a working Monitor step on uploaded actuals; a public-data benchmark module. Also declined: a public-equity analysis tool as a third product, since it sits outside the owner's domain, adds API and licensing dependencies, and drifts toward investment-advice-shaped output.

## 2026-09-14 · IC memo as a PDF deck: two pages, a third for the audit trail
The memo ships as a US Letter landscape PDF rendered on the server with fpdf2, already a dependency. Page one is the recommendation: the checked memo text beside the eight summary figures, sources and uses and the loan. Page two is the evidence: NOI by year, the stress table, the unit mix, the renovation program and exit, and the extracted assumptions with source and confidence. Page three is rendered only for the Screened case: the documents read, every citation, and each question with the extracted figure beside the analyst's answer. The deck draws from the same engine output and memo object as the page, so nothing is retyped. The site typefaces (Source Serif 4 and IBM Plex Sans, both under the SIL Open Font License, bundled in `app/static/fonts/` with their license files) are embedded in the PDF; the core fonts stand in only if the files are missing. Rejected: a PowerPoint export (another dependency, worse tables, and a template look), and a browser print stylesheet (no control over page breaks).

## 2026-09-14 · Bid rule: a 15% levered LP IRR floor, with the bid solved from it
The recommendation now tests the levered LP IRR (the limited partners' return after debt service and the waterfall) against a 15% floor rather than the property-level levered IRR against 12%, since the limited partners' return is what the committee underwrites to. The engine solves the highest purchase price at which the LP IRR still reaches the floor (bisection over the price, with the loan re-sizing and taxes reassessing at each step, to the nearest $10,000) and the verdict follows: bid the underwritten price when it clears; bid no more than the solved price when it does not and that price sits within 20% of the ask; pass otherwise. On the seeded deal the underwritten $46.0M returns 13.0% to the LP, so the memo recommends a bid of no more than $43.7M (13.4% below the $50.5M ask), where the LP IRR is 15.0%, the levered IRR 18.1% and the year 1 DSCR 1.64×. Downside and Lender reach the floor only 28% and 24% below the ask and read as passes. The LP IRR cell on the strip and the deck carries the floor and the maximum bid; the levered IRR is reported without a threshold. Rejected: moving the seed price to the solved bid (a bid that sits exactly on the floor reads as zero cushion, and the point is to show the tool doing the work) and keeping a levered threshold alongside the floor (two rules invite arguments about which one governs).

## 2026-09-14 · Excel exports removed; the land tool gets its own IC memo PDF
Both tools' Excel exports are gone, along with `core/underwriting/excel.py` and `core/copilot/excel.py`. They were faithful dumps of inputs and schedules with live totals, but neither was the working pro forma an analyst would open, and a download that looks like the model without being it is a liability on a public site. In their place the Land Underwriting screen has a "Download IC memo" button producing a two-page PDF on the same primitives as the multifamily deck (`core/pdf.py`): the recommendation beside the eight summary figures, acreage and the land price on page one; net cash flow by year, the sensitivity grid with cells below the floor in red, revenue and cost lines and the active lot mix on page two. The land bid rule is a 15% unlevered IRR floor; the engine solves the highest land price per acre that still reaches it (bisection, nearest $100) and the verdict is bid at the underwritten price, bid no more than the solved price when it sits within 25% of the underwritten one, or pass. On Cypress Ridge the 17.4% IRR clears the floor and the memo says how far the price could rise. The land memo is template-written; a model writer can follow once the multifamily one has settled on live drafts. Rejected: keeping the exports behind a smaller button (the objection was to the artifact, not its prominence).

## 2026-09-14 · Market context from public data, with flags against the assumptions
Both tools gain a Market context panel built from free public sources: FRED for the 10-year Treasury, SOFR, the 30-year mortgage rate, CPI rent inflation, Houston MSA employment and national multifamily permits; the Census Building Permits Survey county files for Harris County 5-plus-unit permits and Waller County single-family permits, as trailing twelve months; and Zillow's Observed Rent Index for Houston. Each row puts the public figure, its date and source beside the assumption it bears on and applies one stated rule (loan rate at least 150 bps over SOFR, the house guideline; exit cap at least 75 bps over it, rent growth no more than 1.5 points above measured rent inflation, permits no more than 25% above the prior year, the land project taking no more than half the county's permitting pace). Fetches run at request time with a six-second timeout and a day-long cache; a failed source falls back to a recorded snapshot and the panel says so, and tests run on the snapshot alone. Declined: paid comp databases (the honest public proxy for cap rates is the Treasury spread), appraisal-district scraping (the tax rate stays an input), and a Monitor step on uploaded actuals (the Copilot decides whether to buy; post-close monitoring is a different job and is removed from the plan). Deferred: HUD Fair Market Rents by ZIP once a HUD User token is set.
